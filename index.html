Why it has to be independent

Your team, our team, an AI. The standard does not move.

So we tested that on ourselves. We pointed the platform at a 1.4 million line ERP maintained on both Oracle and PostgreSQL, and asked it to work out the rules that translate one to the other — once with conventional analysis, once with AI agents.

The AI returned fourteen rules in a single response. One of them corrected 367 files. Another corrupted 86. A third matched nothing at all. Same response, same format, same confidence.

367 files corrected

Its best rule fired on 373 files and improved 367 of them. Exactly what you want from an AI writing your migration.

86 files corrupted

Its worst rule fired on 97 and damaged 86. Well-formed, plausible, and wrong in a way no reviewer catches by reading it.

Both caught

Not by being smarter than the AI. By running every proposed change against the real system and refusing the ones that made things worse.

This is why we are indifferent to who writes the code. Generation is cheap and getting cheaper. Telling a good change from a convincing one is the part that still has to be proved — and it is not something you can ask the writer to do for itself.
